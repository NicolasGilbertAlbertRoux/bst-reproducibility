# Reproducing the projections

Open the simulations tab in the observatory. Select a domain and preparation, then pause at an instant to change its reading. The four reference preparations have English and French GIFs. The language selection covers the labels drawn inside the frames, numerical controls, calculations and measurements tab. It preserves the selected frame. In the molecular reference for excited dicarbon, three successive sets of 72 frames show the same trajectory. Switching to an individual filter retains the corresponding instant and band.

## Entry points

From the repository root, run `python replay.py projections --output ../projections` to rebuild all eight language-specific GIFs, the separate numerical fields and four embedded datasets. The report compares the twelve resulting SHA-256 digests with this edition. Numerical libraries and fonts can affect byte-level identity. The versions in `requirements.txt` and the typefaces in `projections/fonts/` describe the reference environment. The full scientific replay also calls this projection path. For a neutral element, use `python replay.py projections --element Fe --output ../iron`. For the 118 full-sized movies, use `python replay.py export-atlas --output ../atlas` after installing the dependencies in `observatory/package.json`. The repository also includes 118 reduced-size GIFs for immediate inspection. Full-size and compact films preserve the total duration of the selected preparation.

Run `node observatory/check-projections.cjs` with the same Node dependencies to exercise every supported domain, object and reading. Run `node observatory/check-thermal-atlas.cjs` for isotope/ion selection, crystal motifs, camera angles and colour channels. Run `node observatory/check.cjs` for the comparison panel. See the repository README for additional CLI examples.

## Wave and atomic preparations

The two-wave preparation evolves radii and centre separation with a reversible contact law in model time. Kinetic brightness follows moving material, including the centres' motion. The amber channel follows the contact energy and may remain bright at a turning point. White is the arbitrary visual encoding used to make fine motion readable on black. BST proposes that a radiative counterpart at this resolution would lie in the ultraviolet. Speeds and strains alone give no radiation wavelength, power or spectrum. The three reference filters for *excited neutral krypton* refer to separately measured atomic lines. The grey ultraviolet display makes the 123.58 nm channel readable, and the two visible channels use 557.03 and 587.09 nm. These atomic references and the mechanical Kr₂ preparation describe different physical situations.

The neutral catalogue gives an archived count for 86 elements and a conditional core-plus-surface count for 32. In the coherent view each site uses the same phase, with radius r(t) = 0.46 d [1 + 0.28 cos(2πt)], where d is the median nearest-neighbour distance. Peak diameter is 1.28 times its mean and minimum diameter is 0.72 times its mean. The amplitude is selected for legibility. A faint positional marker remains at velocity reversals. A distinct native-mode view uses fixed-size markers to encode modal displacement and velocity. Phase locking is a preparation of the atlas. The archived multimode films retain their measured or calculated phase offsets as specified in their inputs.

For the 36 elements H–Kr, `physical_views.py` forms the Hessian of the archived candidate geometry, removes rigid directions and selects a positive internal mode with the largest radial overlap. Larger display amplitude makes the geometry legible. For the 82 elements Rb–Og, the archived scalar contact mode supplies displacements on the stored outer directions. A complete neutral drawing additionally nests the previous period closure, Kr, Xe or Rn, inside that surface. With N neutral sites, C core sites and unit outer radius, positions in the inherited closure are multiplied by min((C/N)^(1/3), 1 − (4π/(3N))^(1/3)/2). This equal-volume convention preserves the count. It supplies hypothetical interior coordinates. In hydrogen the seventh site similarly occupies an inferred central position. The neutral Kr atlas uses 297 sites. A separate 309-site krypton film replays eight native displacement modes on its own numerical mesh. The isotope and ion selectors open the chosen state as a separate composition card. For an ion, the isotope can be selected or left unspecified. The species selector gives 5,842 nuclear-state rows and 7,021 positive ions. The state-specific physical wave-count column is null for every one of those rows. The card gives the known numbers of protons, neutrons (if A is specified) and bound electrons, with a single frame in both the browser and GIF export. The neutral element count appears as a separate reference. The neutral geometry is accessed through the neutral entry. No state-specific wave count can be inferred from these constituent numbers alone. Export the per-site coordinates, source flags and synchronous radii with `python projections/atlas_geometry.py Og ../og-sites.csv --phase 0.25`. The manifest in `inputs/sources.json` identifies the numerical inputs and their hashes.

## Thermal crystals, cameras and spectroscopy

The solid view starts with a specified periodic structure. The vectors in `crystals.js` are given in ångströms. The centre is O for H₂O, C for CO₂, S for SF₆ and the atomic site for Kr. In ice, the molecule’s two donor directions obey the ice rules on the periodic graph. Each oxygen donates along two edges and accepts along two. The rigid water silhouette keeps its documented molecular angle. In dry ice the four sites carry the distinct body-diagonal axes of Pa-3. In plastic SF₆ the orientations are a seeded snapshot of an orientationally disordered phase.

The four references are at different temperatures. In each model the centres start on the reference sites. If u is displacement, unit masses and unit vector-spring coefficients give acceleration equal to the sum of neighbouring displacements minus the central displacement for each neighbour. A seeded random initial velocity field has zero mean and RMS speed 0.14 Å per reduced time unit. Velocity Verlet uses step 0.025 and records every fourth step for 72 frames. The source checks centre-of-mass drift and energy conservation. The time, force coefficients and amplitudes are illustrative. The calculation describes vibrations around specified sites. It supplies no nucleation trajectory, material phonon spectrum or equilibrium phase boundary. Rebuild it with `python projections/thermal_crystals.py ../crystals.js`.

Azimuth and elevation rotate the centres and molecular silhouettes together with an orthographic projection and equal scales along the three axes. The full crystals share a fixed magnification with a 5 Å scale bar. The first-shell view zooms in on one interior site and its closest neighbours, with a 2 Å scale bar. Dashed segments join the chosen centre to those neighbours. Each fluid view uses one zoom for its box and marks the reduced-to-display length convention. The grayscale intensity follows centre speed with a fixed depth cue. The separate optical panel uses the measured quantity and the preparation below. The two views share identical mechanical coordinates and exposure.

The reported appearances of ordinary unexcited gases under white light are qualitative. Water vapour, CO₂, SF₆ and Kr appear colourless in an ordinary sample. The infrared positions and filtered Kr emission have separate measurement conditions. [NASA water vapour](https://science.nasa.gov/kids/earth/how-do-clouds-form/), [NIOSH CO₂](https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/npg/npgd0103.html), [NIOSH SF₆](https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/npg/npgd0576.html), [Royal Society of Chemistry Kr](https://periodic-table.rsc.org/element/36/krypton/).

The optical path is a separate macroscopic length of 0, 0.01, 0.1, 1, 5 or 10 m. The zero-length cell supplies a white reference. `projections/optics.py` integrates the incident spectrum weighted by transmission and the [CIE 1931 2° observer](https://doi.org/10.25039/CIE.DS.xvudnb9b) under [CIE daylight D65](https://doi.org/10.25039/CIE.DS.hjfjmt59). It uses 1 nm spacing and trapezoidal endpoint weights over the measured interval of 380–700 nm. The transmission is exp(−αL). The water coefficient is linearly interpolated between measurements. For ice, interpolate log(k) against log(wavelength) and calculate α = 4πk/λ with λ in metres. Linear sRGB channels are balanced once against the incident white, clipped to the screen gamut and encoded with the sRGB transfer function. Relative luminance uses the Y integral before clipping. Screen brightness, glare, scattering and the observer's adaptation can affect an actual viewing experiment.

The water, ice and observer CSV files in `projections/inputs/optics/` contain the observer and illuminant on the common grid, the measured water coefficients with uncertainties, and the ice extinction indices bracketing the display interval. The first CSV combines the visible subset of the two CIE datasets above, © CIE, licensed under [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/). The original observer and illuminant files have MD5 identifiers `17cca777db64b17170f06f67ce9d3ab7` and `03d4eb9b837c60671627c946fb534deb`. The optical replay serves the tabulated RGB values to both renderers. Exported GIFs reserve the measured colour swatches in their palettes.

## Calculating a diffuse moving boundary

1. Deposit the instantaneous 3D positions of **all** sites of each atom on a local 59³ grid. Smooth the density with a Gaussian of standard deviation 0.76 or 1.02 median nearest-neighbour spacings. For each atom and smoothing width, define the reference surface density as the median density at the native surface markers in the undeformed preparation. The median sets a density threshold without prescribing symmetry or a radial profile.
2. Fit a weighted affine velocity gradient in the physical neighbourhood of each site using the instantaneous positions and velocities of both atoms. Rigid translation and rotation drop out of the symmetric part of that gradient. Use the Frobenius norm of this strain-rate tensor as the local activity. An isolated, uniformly translating rigid body carries no signal in this channel. Positive and negative local dilatation of equal magnitude can both light the mantle.
3. At each voxel divide the smoothed activity-weighted density by the smoothed density. Multiply by a diffuse band about the fixed reference density. For density ratio `s`, the band weight is `exp(-0.5*((s-1)/w)^2) * min(s,1)^2`, with `w` of 0.20 and 0.265 for the two spatial readings. Then integrate along the viewing axis. Both atoms retain one connected density core at both smoothing widths throughout the individual records.
4. At fine resolution the grey channel shows the speed of the sites relative to their own atomic centre. The molecular reading adds up to 16% of this channel to the narrower mantle. The coarsest reading uses only the wider mantle. Fixed exposure across all frames of a species makes time comparisons possible. Each optical filter replays the identical C₂ trajectory for comparison. The band heads at 473.7, 516.5 and 563.5 nm correspond to (1,0), (0,0) and (0,1), respectively. The three filters sample more than one upper vibrational level. Brightness uses normalized display units, without an absolute intensity or a measured band ratio.

The file `mantle-verification.json` reports energy conservation, carbon force/Hessian consistency, diffusion widths, topology checks, boost invariance and synthetic rigid-rotation tests. Connectivity is tested separately on each model atom at both resolutions and all 72 frames. This tests the numerical reading of an individual mantle even when the two projected mantles overlap on the screen.

## Spectroscopy and physical scope

The archived Kr pair calculation gives an equilibrium separation of 4.0193 Å, a 1.65475 ps radial period and an energy above the potential minimum of 4.21164 meV. The film visualizes mechanical deformation. The green C₂ band instead corresponds to light of about 2.40047 eV per quantum. The spatial profile and local changes in brightness within C₂ are a BST visualization hypothesis. No electronic populations, transition dipoles or far-field image of a resolved individual molecule are calculated. A change of viewing resolution changes the projection of the data. An assertion of electromagnetic emission also requires a source and a spectral channel.

In the isolated excited Kr film, the bright fine sites trace motion. Alongside them the UV line at 123.58378 nm in vacuum is displayed in neutral grey, and the visible lines at 557.02894 nm and 587.09160 nm in air are displayed in approximate screen colours. The three ideal unresolved images use a common circular aperture, with normalized brightness and a fixed display gain. Their diffraction widths scale with wavelength. The three filters describe separate observations. They do not spatially resolve which site emits. The thermal and cosmological preparations are described below.


## Molecular, thermal, field and cosmological views

- **H₂O and CO₂.** Equilibrium geometry and gas-phase vibrational frequencies are external inputs. Bending and stretching are illustrated with small prescribed displacements; the mass-weighted translation is removed every frame. Water offers three readings and CO₂ two. The rendered atoms use the neutral atlas shapes. H, C, O, F and S adopt additive covalent radii of 0.31, 0.76, 0.66, 0.57 and 1.05 Å, respectively. All three molecules share a view scale of 68 pixels per ångström. The radius convention describes diffuse boundaries; the reference values carry experimental spread. The internal silhouettes show motion in grey. At molecular resolution the optical panel places measured gas-phase absorption bands on an infrared wavelength axis. Their positions alone give no visible mantle colour.
- **SF₆.** Six bond directions and the selected archived mode set the display geometry. The selector covers all 66 directions of the contact operator. The spectroscopy panel independently shows the measured infrared absorption.
- **Reduced fluid and ordered comparators.** `physical_views.py` propagates 108 equal-mass centres in a periodic cubic cell. The force-shifted Lennard-Jones potential has a cutoff of min(2.5, 0.49 times the cell side). Velocity Verlet uses a reduced time step of 0.003. After 550 equilibration steps, 72 samples are recorded at intervals of 15 steps. The three (density, target temperature, seed) triples are (1.03, 0.26, 918), (0.82, 0.90, 919) and (0.065, 1.30, 920). Mean-square displacement uses unwrapped trajectories. Instantaneous temperature, virial pressure and density use reduced units. H₂O, CO₂ and SF₆ use the same radius and equilibrium-geometry functions as the isolated-molecule view. A single zoom factor for each thermal cell acts on all atoms and bonds alike. The silhouettes are rigid and rotate illustratively. The particle-centre model has isotropic forces for every silhouette. Separate material coexistence curves are available under measurements.
- **Magnetic flux.** For each of 66 SF₆ modes, differentiate the calibrated electric dipole P = Σ [a(r−r₀)+br₀] u along the archived displacement, with r₀ fixed at its equilibrium reference. The reader verifies the squared derivative magnitudes against the archived IR intensities for each of two fits. The relative exterior map shows the line-of-sight component of (dP/dt) × r / |r|³, from the electric dipole near-field approximation. One common reference amplitude per fit sets the exposure. The central exclusion covers the molecular source. The fitted intensity does not fix a global sign or an absolute magnetic field in tesla. The six independent archived current runs give J and transverse power at nine times, four directions and four wave numbers. Three fixed-dipole scenes remain available as classical comparators. No local measurement of magnetic adhesion is supplied.
- **Material response.** Each stiffness (1, 2, 4) and inertia (4, 8, 16) selects its archived integration. The shape, scalar and charge profiles correspond to a common time sample. Under measurements, the joint parameter register lists the candidate combinations that satisfy the selected constraints.
- **Cosmology.** One seeded Gaussian temperature realization (seed 29113) uses the TT band powers of the selected CAMB family. Interpolation is restricted to multipoles 48–84. Seven Gaussian beams from 0° to 3° FWHM use those same random coefficients and the same map colour scale across beam widths. C00, the selected family and their signed difference use the same phases and beam. The first two panels use ±85 µK. The residual uses ±5 µK and reports its area-weighted RMS, including the zero self-comparison. Varying the beam gives the effect of angular resolution on a synthetic sky. The Planck TT, TE, EE band comparisons and the family spectra remain separately available. The frame index is beam width rather than elapsed cosmic time.

## Gravitating molecular aggregates

Choose two or three homogeneous spherical bodies. Each body has a composition (¹H₂¹⁶O, ¹²C¹⁶O₂, ³²S¹⁹F₆ or ⁸⁴Kr), radius R in kilometres and constituent number density n in units of 10²⁸ m⁻³. The neutral isotope masses come from the distributed catalogue. Multiplying n × 4πR³/3 by the constituent mass gives the total mass. This calculation neglects molecular binding energy. Radius and density are chosen independently of the thermal states.

The centres move under Newtonian mutual attraction with G = 6.67430 × 10⁻¹¹ m³ kg⁻¹ s⁻². The solver uses the centre-of-mass frame and velocity Verlet with 60-second steps. It records one point per hour and the first surface contact, located by intersection along the last position step. Integration ends at contact or 96 hours. The experiment tests a macroscopic gravitational limit. Its masses use the catalogue, while its gravitational law and initial conditions are explicit inputs. It does not evolve the internal waves of each macroscopic body or predict a star's chemical composition.

```sh
python projections/check_gravity.py
python projections/gravity.py binary ../binary-orbit.csv
python projections/gravity.py triple ../three-bodies.csv --substeps 120
python observatory/export_gif.py gravity binary ../binary.gif --body1 SF6 --radius1 1800
python observatory/export_gif.py field SF6 ../induction.gif --mode 11
python observatory/export_gif.py sky C11 ../family-difference.gif
```
